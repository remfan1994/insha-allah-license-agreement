## 1. ALLAH

### 1.1 Allah Is Supreme

Allah is supreme, omniscient, and omnipotent.

The Licensor, the Licensee, the Licensed Material, its use, and every consequence of its use already exist under Allah.

This Agreement does not establish that condition. It remembers and describes it.

### 1.2 Consciousness

Consciousness is for the MOST MERCY.

Consciousness knows, witnesses, measures, anticipates, experiences, and recognizes consequence.

Mercy gives purpose to beneficence.

### 1.3 Algebra - Al-Jabbar

Algebra is the ordered relation of measure, proportion, cause, consequence, exchange, persistence, transformation, and return.

Algebra provides the MOST BENEFICENCE.

Allah is Al-Jabbar, the Irresistible.

Reality does not stop being real because a person would prefer another result. Action enters relation and consequence.

Consciousness measures and preempts the Algebra. The Algebra exists for Consciousness.

Beneficence has no legitimate purpose except mercy.

Mercy has no practical operation except through beneficence.

The IALA recognizes this Conscious Algebra under Allah.

## 3. DHIKR AND SHARING

### 3.1 Remembrance

The mention of Allah in this Agreement is dhikr.

Sharing Material and remembering Allah have a useful correspondence.

Sharing makes something available that another consciousness may use.

Remembrance makes a consciousness attentive to what Allah has made available.

Neither guarantees an outcome.

Both open opportunity.

### 3.2 Gratitude

We give shukr to Allah for the ability to share useful Material and for the inclination to share it.

Allah is As-Shakur.

The IALA therefore approaches useful sharing with gratitude.

If the Material benefits another consciousness, that opportunity came through Allah.

If another consciousness improves it and shares it again, further opportunity has been opened.

### 3.3 No Obligation Created by Dhikr

Mentioning Allah does not make the Licensor responsible for the Licensee.

It does not make the Licensee responsible for the Licensor.

The remembrance describes the reality under which the sharing occurs.

It does not transfer one person's burden to another.

## 4. SEPARATE BURDENS

### 4.1 Each Actor Bears Their Own Action

The Licensor bears the Licensor's actions.

The Licensee bears the Licensee's actions.

Sharing Material does not merge those actions.

Receiving Material does not merge those actions.

One person's independent conduct does not become another person's conduct merely because Material passed between them.

### 4.2 The Licensor Is Not the Licensee's Keeper

By sharing the Material, the Licensor does not become the Licensee's:

- guardian;

- agent;

### 4.3 Enabling Is Not Performing

Making Material available may enable another person's action.

That does not by itself make the sharer the performer of the later action.

The later actor remains the actor.

Cause, knowledge, intention, circumstance, and consequence remain what they actually are.

The IALA does not invent liability merely because two people are connected by shared Material.

## 8. FIQH COMMENTARY

This section is commentary on the IALA. It does not create the freedoms above and does not restrict them.

### 8.1 Measure

Quran 54:49 states that everything is created according to measure.

Quran 55:7-9 speaks of the balance and of maintaining proper measure.

The IALA's language of Algebra, measure, relation, and consequence is consistent with these observations.

### 8.2 Separate Burdens

Quran 6:164 states that no bearer of burdens bears the burden of another.

This closely corresponds to the IALA's description of Licensor and Licensee as separate actors.

### 8.3 Fitrah

Quran 30:30 speaks of the fitrah upon which people are created.

The IALA accordingly does not make knowledge of Allah depend upon Arabic terminology while still treating reality, consciousness, measure, and consequence as unavoidable.

### 8.4 Gratitude

Quran 14:7 connects gratitude with increase.

The IALA gives shukr for the ability and inclination to share useful Material.

### 8.5 Beneficial Knowledge

Sahih Muslim reports beneficial knowledge among the things whose benefit can continue after a person's death.

The IALA sees useful sharing as an opportunity for benefit to continue beyond the first sharer.

These observations confirm and comment upon the IALA's position. They are not the source from which Allah is derived.
